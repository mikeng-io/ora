# Recording the demo — for Jimmy and Alfred

You don't need to know anything about how Ora works. You need to do two
things well today: **send a handful of exact messages at the right moments**,
and **capture clean screen recordings of your phones while you do it.** This
page tells you exactly how.

Read this whole page once before you touch anything. Then do the "before
you record" section, then follow the run-of-show table.

## The idea, in one paragraph

Ora doesn't live on a website or an app you'd show a judge — it lives
*inside* WhatsApp and Signal, answering as if it were just another person in
the group. That means a plain screen recording of your phone, showing the
real chat, is not a fallback for not having a laptop — **it's the truest
possible footage of the product**, maybe better than a laptop screen would
be. Don't feel like you're doing the "phone version" of the demo. You're
doing the real one.

## Who does what

Two phones, two apps, both recording for the entire run, start to finish.

- **Jimmy — records WhatsApp.** You also send every scripted WhatsApp
  message (steps 1 and 5 below).
- **Alfred — records Signal.** You don't send any scripted message until
  step 6 — your job for most of the run is to hold the camera (so to
  speak) on Signal and catch Ora speaking on its own.
- **Mike** sends one message himself, on Signal (step 2, the one where he
  tags Ora). You don't need to do anything for that one except keep
  recording.

Both of you start recording at the same time and keep rolling for the
**whole run, about 5 minutes**, even through the parts where nothing is
happening. Never stop and restart between steps — a cut in the middle is
exactly what would make this look staged, and the entire point is that it
isn't.

## 1. Before you record: set up your phone

Do this on **both** phones, about 5 minutes before the real take.

### Turn on Do Not Disturb

This stops other notifications from popping up and covering the chat while
you're recording — a text from your mum, an Instagram like, anything. It
also matters because Jimmy's WhatsApp phone can still get a *Signal*
notification banner (and vice versa) if Do Not Disturb isn't on.

- **iPhone:** Control Center (swipe down from the top-right corner) → tap
  the crescent-moon **Focus** button → **Do Not Disturb**.
- **Android:** swipe down twice from the top → tap **Do Not Disturb**.
  (If you don't see the tile, swipe down twice, tap the pencil/edit icon,
  and drag "Do Not Disturb" into your quick settings.)

Do Not Disturb does **not** stop you from using the app you're already in
— it just silences everything else, including phone calls, so a call
doesn't cut the recording off in the middle.

### Turn off auto-lock (screen timeout) for now

There's a stretch in the middle of the run where **nothing happens on
purpose for about a minute and a half.** If your phone's screen times out
and locks during that quiet stretch, your recording stops. Turn this off
before you start, and turn it back on afterward.

- **iPhone:** Settings → Display & Brightness → Auto-Lock → **Never**.
- **Android:** Settings → Display → Screen timeout → the longest option
  available (e.g. 30 minutes).

### Orientation: portrait, not landscape

Keep the phone upright the whole time. That's how WhatsApp and Signal are
actually used, it's what will look natural in the video, and honestly
most phones won't let you rotate a chat screen into landscape anyway.
Don't fight it — just hold it normally.

### Brightness

This one's a bit counterintuitive: a screen recording captures the exact
pixels on your screen, not a photo of it, so brightness doesn't actually
change how the recording looks. Turn it up anyway, just so **you** can see
clearly what you're doing and don't fumble a tap.

### Keep the clock visible

Don't do anything that hides your status bar (no "immersive" mode, don't
scroll the chat so far that the time disappears from a full-screen app).
The little clock at the top of your screen is how Mike will be able to line
up your two recordings — and his own — afterward. Just leave it alone and
it'll be there.

### Get your chats ready

- Open the right group in the right app.
- Scroll to the very bottom so you're looking at the live end of the
  conversation.
- **Type your first scripted message into the message box, but do not send
  it yet.** That way, when it's time, sending is one single tap — no
  fumbling with a keyboard on camera. As soon as you send one, type the
  next one you're responsible for so it's ready and waiting.

### Turn your microphone off for the screen recording

You don't need to narrate anything — Mike is handling that separately.
Recording audio just risks picking up room noise or you three talking
about the setup. When you start the recording (steps below), pick **no
sound / mic off**.

## 2. How to actually screen-record

### iPhone

1. Add the screen recorder if it isn't already there: **Settings → Control
   Center → tap the ➕ next to "Screen Recording."**
2. Open **Control Center**: swipe down from the top-right corner of the
   screen (or up from the bottom edge, on an iPhone with a Home button).
3. **Tap the round Screen Recording icon** (looks like a solid dot inside a
   circle). Do **not** press and hold — a normal tap starts it with the
   microphone off, which is what you want.
4. Wait for the 3-2-1 countdown. Recording has started when a red pill
   appears at the top of your screen.
5. Go back to your chat app and follow the run-of-show below.
6. **To stop:** tap the red pill/time at the top of the screen and confirm
   **Stop**, or reopen Control Center and tap the recording icon again.
7. The video saves automatically to your **Photos** app, usually under
   "Recents" or an album called "Screen Recordings."

### Android

Exact menus vary a little by phone brand, but the shape is the same:

1. Swipe down from the top of the screen **twice** to open the full quick
   settings panel.
2. Find the **Screen Record** tile. If you don't see it, tap the pencil /
   edit icon and drag "Screen Recorder" into your active tiles — or search
   "screen recorder" in the Settings app.
3. Tap it. You'll be asked about audio — choose **"No audio" / "Mic off."**
4. Tap **Start**. Wait for the countdown.
5. Go back to your chat app and follow the run-of-show below.
6. **To stop:** pull down the notification shade and tap **Stop** on the
   screen recorder notification, or open quick settings and tap the tile
   again.
7. The video saves to your **Photos/Gallery** app, usually in an album
   called "Screen recordings."

**Do a 10-second test recording on both phones before the real take**,
just to confirm you know where the button is and where the file lands.

## 3. The run-of-show

Times are roughly minutes:seconds from when the first message goes out —
**not exact.** Ora can take a few extra seconds to answer, and that's
normal, not a sign something's broken. Wait it out.

**Important: Mike will call out loud when it's time for each of your
messages — "Jimmy, go" or "Alfred, go."** Don't try to watch a clock and
guess the moment yourself. And whichever phone you're not sending from:
just keep holding still and recording.

**Type each message exactly as written below.** Don't reword it, even if
it feels more natural to phrase it differently — the exact wording is part
of what makes Ora respond the way it's supposed to. If typing Chinese
characters is awkward on your keyboard, ask Mike to send you the two lines
you're responsible for as a text beforehand so you can copy-paste them into
the message box ahead of time (per the "type it, don't send it yet" step
above).

| ~time | who | app | do this | (in English) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | **Jimmy** | WhatsApp | Send: `聽日幾點去 Cyberport？` | "What time are we going to Cyberport tomorrow?" |
| 0:00–0:40 | Alfred | Signal | Just watch and record. Nobody sent this on Signal — don't reply to it there. | — |
| 0:40 | **Mike** | Signal | Sends his own tagged message. You don't do anything — keep recording. | (he's asking what time + how to get there) |
| ~1:00 | *nobody* | both | **Silent step.** Send nothing on either phone. This is Ora quietly closing something on its own — the silence is the whole point. | — |
| 1:00–2:30 | *nobody* | both, especially Signal | **The big one. Send nothing for up to about 90 seconds.** Keep both phones recording, screens on, don't touch anything. Somewhere in this window, Ora will send a message **on Signal, on its own, without anyone asking.** | — |
| — | Alfred | Signal | When Ora's message appears: **don't scroll, don't switch apps, don't tap anything.** Just hold the phone still with the message on screen for a good 5 seconds before doing anything else. | — |
| ~2:40 | **Jimmy** | WhatsApp | Send: `唔使諗啦，聽日唔食飯，改咗下星期` | "Never mind — no dinner tomorrow, we moved it to next week." |
| 2:40–3:30 | *nobody* | especially Signal | Watch Signal. Ora should say **nothing** here — the point of this step is that it quietly cancels its own earlier plan without announcing it. Don't send anything, don't comment. | — |
| ~3:30 | **Jimmy & Alfred** | WhatsApp + Signal | Now send a bit of ordinary chatter on your own phone — a link, a couple of "lol"s, anything low-key. Doesn't need to match any script. This is meant to look like normal banter. | — |
| 3:30–4:00 | *nobody* | both | Keep recording a little longer after the banter. Ora should stay quiet through this too — that's the last beat. | — |
| ~4:00–4:15 | — | — | Stop recording on both phones. | — |

A few things worth repeating because they're easy to get wrong under
pressure:

- **During a silent step, silence means silence.** No "is this working?",
  no checking in with each other in the chat, no thumbs-up emoji. A
  message sent out of nerves during the quiet stretch ruins that shot
  completely — there's no editing it out afterward.
- If you're not sure whether it's your turn: **it isn't, unless Mike just
  said your name.** When in doubt, don't send anything.
- Don't react out loud in a way that would make you laugh or fumble the
  phone right as Ora's message lands — keep it steady for those few extra
  seconds after it appears.

## 4. Getting the footage to Mike

**Do not send the video through WhatsApp or Signal as a normal video
attachment.** Both apps compress video heavily before sending, and the
footage will come out blurry and hard to read — exactly the opposite of
what we need. Use one of these instead, in order of preference:

1. **AirDrop (iPhone only, and only if Mike has a Mac nearby).** Open
   Photos, select your recording, tap the share icon, choose **AirDrop**,
   tap Mike's Mac. Full quality, instant, no upload needed. This is the
   best option if it's available.
2. **Google Photos or Google Drive.** Open the app, upload the video (it
   may already be auto-backing up — check), then get a shareable link and
   send *the link* (not the file) to Mike by text or email. Full quality,
   works from either an iPhone or an Android phone.
3. **A cable, straight into Mike's computer.** If you have the right cable
   and Mike is set up to receive it (Finder on a Mac for an iPhone, or an
   Android file-transfer app), this also preserves full quality.

If for some reason none of these are possible and you truly must send it
through a chat app: use the **"Document" / "File"** attachment option
instead of "Photo" or "Video" — sending it as a document usually skips the
compression. But treat this as a last resort, not a default.

## 5. If something goes wrong

Don't panic, don't retype the same message twice trying to "fix" it, and
don't stop and restart the whole recording unless the list below says so.
The general rule: **keep recording, don't delete anything, tell Mike
afterward which rough time it happened.**

| what happens | what you do |
|---|---|
| Ora doesn't say anything when it's supposed to | Keep recording. Don't send a "hello?" or "is it working" message. Wait it out — an extra 30–45 seconds is fine. Tell Mike the time; this might just mean that step gets cut later, and that's his call, not something you need to fix live. |
| Ora says something strange or wrong | Keep recording. Don't reply to it, don't react in the chat, don't delete it. Just note the time and mention it to Mike after. |
| A message fails to send | Try sending the exact same text again, once. Keep recording the whole time. Tell Mike roughly when this happened. |
| A phone call comes in and interrupts the recording | Do Not Disturb should mostly prevent this. If a call still gets through: don't answer it. If the recording actually stopped, restart it immediately, note the gap, and flag to Mike that this run may need a redo — a recording with a real gap in it usually can't be used for the main shot. |
| You're not sure if it's your turn | It isn't. Wait for Mike to say your name. |

## 6. For Mike: what your teammates' phones can't get you

The old plan for this video assumed one continuous laptop screen recording
(Signal Desktop + WhatsApp Web + a terminal window, side by side, with you
narrating live). None of that is what's happening now — the footage is
coming from two phones. That's a real strength for the chat itself, but it
means a few things the original storyboard and capture checklist expected
are simply not obtainable from a phone, and they're now yours to capture
separately, from your own machine:

- **Every `loop_tail` terminal shot.** This is the log output — `GATE ·`,
  `TURN ·`, `FOLD ·`, `CURATION ·`, `PROACTIVE ·`, the `no_go` lines. None
  of this appears anywhere in the chat apps; it's the only thing that
  proves an answer was grounded, a note was closed citing a human, an
  intention was formed before it fired, or a silence was a judgement and
  not just nothing happening. Per `docs/capture-checklist.md`, this is
  what several of the "must-have" crops (steps 3, 4, 5, and 6) are built
  from — you'll need your own screen recording of it, timed alongside the
  phones (start it at the same moment as the phones, and keep the clock
  visible on your screen the same way the phones are keeping theirs
  visible, so you can line the three recordings up afterward).
- **The title card and the end-card diagram.** `docs/video-storyboard.md`
  and `docs/capture-checklist.md` both assume these two 1920×1080 PNGs
  exist as separate rendered assets, originally assigned to "teammate B."
  That role doesn't exist on this team — these are on you (or whoever else
  you can pull in), not something a phone recording produces.
- **The `uv run pytest -q` passing screenshot** and any shot of the
  `notes` / `reminders` tables in their end state (N1 closed, N2 moot).
  Both are nice-to-have per the checklist, both come from your machine.
- **The voice-over.** The original plan was live narration over one
  continuous take. With three separate recordings (two phones, your log),
  there's no single take to narrate live over anymore — you'll need to
  decide whether to record narration separately over the edited-together
  footage, or skip it. That's a production decision now, not something
  that happens automatically.

Everything else in `docs/video-storyboard.md` about the Mac three-pane
layout (Signal Desktop, WhatsApp Web, terminal, all in one window) no
longer applies — that was written for a laptop-only capture plan. The chat
footage is coming from the phones now; only the log and the static assets
above still need to come from you.
